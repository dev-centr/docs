= Subscription management platforms
:navtitle: Subscription management platforms
:description: Explanation of in-app subscription infrastructure (Adapty, RevenueCat, and related tools), access levels versus store SKUs, and how they relate to Stripe and static/web SaaS sites.

Thin SaaS products and mobile apps often need the same answer on every surface: **does this customer currently have paid access?**
That problem is usually called **subscription management**, **in-app subscription infrastructure**, or (in vendor APIs) **entitlements** / **access levels** — not a separate product category branded “entitlement layer.”

This page is architecture advice for web and hybrid teams. It complements xref:architecture/ui-heavy-web-apps.adoc[UI-heavy web applications].

== The problem Stripe alone does not solve

link:https://stripe.com/docs/customer-management[Stripe Customer Portal] is excellent for **Stripe** invoices, payment methods, and cancellations.
It does **not** show App Store, Google Play, or Microsoft Store subscriptions.
Apple and Google require their own in-app purchase rails for digital goods inside mobile apps; you cannot route those through Stripe.

So a product that sells on **web + mobile** needs:

. A **payment rail** per channel (Stripe Checkout, StoreKit, Play Billing, …)
. A **subscription management platform** (or your own backend) that normalizes those rails into one access check

== Common terminology

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| Phrase you will hear | What it means

| Subscription management platform / in-app subscription infrastructure
| Hosted service that validates receipts, tracks renewals/refunds/grace periods, and exposes “has access?” APIs (e.g. Adapty, RevenueCat, Qonversion)

| Access level (Adapty) / entitlement (RevenueCat)
| Logical unlock (`premium`, `instalay`), mapped from one or more store product IDs

| Offerings / paywalls / placements
| What the user is shown to buy — often A/B tested; orthogonal to the access check itself

| Membership platform (Memberstack, Outseta)
| Login + gating for **websites**, usually Stripe-backed — not a substitute for App Store IAP

| Customer portal
| UI for managing billing with **one** merchant (Stripe Portal, store subscription settings)
|===

Engineers sometimes say “entitlement layer” for the middle box.
Prefer **subscription management** or **access levels** when writing docs or searching videos (`RevenueCat vs Adapty`, `in-app subscription SDK`).

**Superwall** (and similar) optimize **paywall presentation**.
They are usually paired with Adapty or RevenueCat, not used as the sole source of subscription truth.

== Web + mobile with one customer id

Typical pattern:

. Authenticate the user (or collect a stable buyer email).
. On Stripe Checkout, set metadata `customer_user_id` to that id (and enable invoice creation for one-time payments if the platform requires invoice events).
. In the mobile app, call the SDK `identify` with the **same** id after login.
. Gate features on an **access level**, not on raw product IDs.

Without a shared id, web purchase and mobile install become two anonymous profiles.

== Practical guidance

* If the product is **desktop/web license keys first** and mobile IAP is future work, keep a simple license table (or key format) and add Adapty/RevenueCat when store IAPs ship — still design the **customer id** early.
* If the product is **mobile-subscription-first** and conversion testing is the growth lever, **Adapty** is a strong forward-looking default; **RevenueCat** remains the ecosystem default.
* Do not expect one merchant portal to list every store’s invoices; unify on **access status** (and optionally a thin “My account” page that lists sources).
* Keep **tenant membership** (team roles) separate from **paid access** in multi-tenant apps.
